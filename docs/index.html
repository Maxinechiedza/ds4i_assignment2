3 Evaluation design

TipOwner: Maxine (Role A) · Task 4 · ≈ 550 words

The brief asks for: the chosen train, validation and test strategy and why · what kind of generalisation the evaluation represents · possible leakage or optimistic performance.

Answer these:

  • Main split (both models): sessions 1–3 train, 4 validation, 5 test. What does it test? (A known writer on a new day.)
  • Unseen-writer split (Siamese only): 14 / 3 / 3 writers. What does it test? (A person the model has never seen.) Why can the CNN not do this?
  • What is validation used for (early stopping, tuning, the Siamese threshold)? Why is test touched only once?
  • Optimism experiment: how much better does a simple baseline look on a random image split than on the session-held-out split?
  • Leakage risks: near-duplicate replicates within a session, pairs built before splitting, a threshold tuned on test, the same image in training and test pairs.
  • Why might results still be optimistic even with a clean split (simulated data, 20 writers)?

Exhibits: tbl-split-counts images per split · tbl-split-optimism (or fig-split-optimism) baseline accuracy on the two splits.

Reads: data/splits.csv. Code: R/00_data.R writes the split; R/optimism_baseline.R runs the experiment.

5 Same-writer matching with a Siamese network

ImportantOwner: Role C · Task 3 · ≈ 650 words

The brief asks for: a Siamese network that estimates whether two images come from the same writer · contrastive or triplet loss · how training examples are constructed · how matching performance is evaluated.

Answer these:

  • Architecture: shared encoder (same backbone as the CNN), embedding size, distance.
  • Loss: contrastive or triplet, the margin, and why.
  • Training examples: how positive and negative pairs are sampled, why digit is held fixed, the same/different balance, cross-session positives, and pairs built only after splitting.
  • Threshold: chosen on validation pairs by balanced accuracy.
  • Test results on data/test_pairs.csv: ROC AUC, equal error rate, balanced accuracy, and distance histograms for same vs different pairs.
  • Unseen-writer experiment: the same metrics on the 3 held-out writers.
  • What the errors mean: a false accept matches two different writers; a false reject misses a true match. Which matters more depends on the use.

Exhibits: fig-siam-pairs example positive and negative pairs · fig-siam-dist distance histograms · fig-siam-roc ROC curves · tbl-siam-metrics main split vs unseen writers.

Reads: outputs/siamese_test_distances.csv, outputs/siamese_history.csv. Code: R/siamese.R (or python/siamese.py).
